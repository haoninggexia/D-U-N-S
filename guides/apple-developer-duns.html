D-U-N-S Number for the Apple Developer Program

Enrolling as an organization means Apple verifies your legal entity against the Dun & Bradstreet database. Here is exactly what it checks, how long each route takes, and why a valid-looking number still gets rejected.

D-U-N-S Helper · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read

Why Apple asks for a D-U-N-S Number

When you enroll in the Apple Developer Program as an organization rather than an individual, Apple has to confirm that the entity signing its legal agreements actually exists. Instead of building its own worldwide company registry, Apple validates your details against the Dun & Bradstreet (D&B) database using the nine-digit D-U-N-S Number.

Apple is explicit about who qualifies: your business must be a legal entity (a corporation, limited liability company, limited partnership and so on). DBA names, trade names, fictitious businesses and branches are not accepted. Individual developer accounts do not need a D-U-N-S Number at all. Government organizations may opt out.

This is why the number is a gating item: no valid, matching D-U-N-S record means no organization account, which means no App Store publishing under a company name and no team member management.

Where the number actually comes from

The D-U-N-S Number is assigned and maintained by Dun & Bradstreet, not by Apple. Apple's own support page tells you to look up your organization first — D&B may already have assigned one for free — and only submit a request if no record is found. The lookup asks for the legal entity name, headquarters address, mailing address and a work contact.

Most teams skip this step. They assume they need a brand-new number, submit a fresh application, and then wait weeks for D&B to re-verify a record that may already exist. Checking first is the single highest-leverage thing you can do.

Apple's Chinese support page adds one detail many paid "expediting" offers quietly omit: expediting the D-U-N-S creation process does not shorten the wait Apple describes. In other words, paying to rush a form does not necessarily move you to the front of the queue.

Even after you have the nine digits, allow time for the record to propagate before Apple can validate it, and remember Apple runs its own review on top. Budget a buffer rather than planning a launch around a best case.

Why Apple rejects a number that looks valid

  • Name mismatch. The legal name in your Apple enrollment must match the D&B record. A translated name, an abbreviation or a pinyin-only version is a classic cause of "unable to verify".
  • Address mismatch. A D-U-N-S Number identifies an entity at a specific location. Using a headquarters number for a branch, or an old registered address, can fail validation.
  • Wrong entity type. Sole proprietors, DBAs and branches are not accepted for organization enrollment.
  • Someone else's number. Copying a parent company's or a partner's D-U-N-S will not pass.
  • Too fresh. A newly created record may not yet be visible to Apple's verification system.
  • Unreachable contact. D&B may phone to confirm business type, headcount or registration documents. A missed call restarts the clock.

A checklist before you press Enroll

  1. Search for an existing record under the exact registered legal name.
  2. Confirm the English legal name, street address, city, province/state and postcode match your registration documents character for character.
  3. Use a work email and a phone number that will actually be answered during business hours.
  4. Confirm the entity type is a legal entity, not a DBA or branch.
  5. Have the business registration document ready in case D&B calls.
  6. Wait for the record to sync before submitting the Apple form; do not resubmit repeatedly.

Do not submit duplicate applications. A second request while the first is pending can freeze the case or create conflicting records — the opposite of what you need when you have a deadline.

If you are blocked right now

Start by establishing which of two situations you are in: the record exists but you cannot find it, or it genuinely does not exist yet. The fixes are completely different. An existing-record lookup is usually fast; a genuinely new D&B record is bound by D&B's own verification timeline and no third party can honestly promise otherwise.

Frequently asked questions

Is a D-U-N-S Number required for an individual Apple Developer account?

No. It is only required when enrolling as an organization (company or educational institution). Individual accounts do not need one.

How long until Apple can validate my D-U-N-S Number?

D&B may take up to 5 business days to issue a number through Apple's channel, and a newly created record needs time to propagate before Apple's verification can see it. Apple then runs its own review.

Can I use my parent company's D-U-N-S Number?

No. Apple validates the legal entity that is signing the agreement. A number belonging to a different entity or a different physical location will be rejected.

Is the Apple D-U-N-S Number different from a normal one?

No. There is one D&B database and one number per entity per location. The same number is used for Apple, Google Play, supplier onboarding and tenders.

Does paying D&B for expedited service guarantee a faster Apple approval?

D&B's expedited option shortens its own processing to about 8 business days, but Apple states that expediting the D-U-N-S creation process does not shorten the wait it describes. Apple's review is separate.
