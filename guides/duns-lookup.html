How to Find a D-U-N-S Number You Already Have

Dun & Bradstreet assigns D-U-N-S Numbers while building its business database. Many companies discover they have had one for years. Here is where to look, how to verify it is the right one, and what to do if the search returns nothing.

D-U-N-S Helper · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

Why you may already have one

A D-U-N-S Number is not something a business necessarily "applies for". It is D&B's identifier for a legal entity at a specific location, created as part of maintaining a global business database. Records are frequently created from public registrations, credit and trade data, or because a customer, lender or platform requested verification. The owner is rarely notified.

D&B's own guidance says: before you register, check whether you already have one. It will not issue a duplicate. Apple gives the same advice for organization enrollment.

Six places to look before you apply

  1. D&B's free D-U-N-S lookup tool — search by legal name, address or phone. Try both the full legal name and any historical name.
  2. Apple's D-U-N-S lookup during organization enrollment — it searches the same D&B data and can surface the number directly.
  3. Old emails from D&B — search your inbox and the previous owner's/accountant's inbox for "D-U-N-S", "D&B" or a nine-digit number.
  4. Vendor onboarding paperwork — supplier questionnaires, W-9 equivalents, credit applications and procurement portals often record it.
  5. Invoices, contracts and tender documents — especially from multinationals, US government-related work or international logistics.
  6. Your company website footer or About page — some firms publish their DUNS for credibility.

Search by address and phone as well as by name. A lookup commonly fails because the name on file uses a different English translation or a former registered name, while the address still matches.

How to verify the number is the right one

Finding nine digits is not the same as having a usable number. Check four things:

  • Entity: the record belongs to the legal entity that will sign the agreement, not a parent, subsidiary, branch or former company.
  • Location: D-U-N-S is assigned per physical location. A different registered address may need a different number.
  • Name and address format: what is in D&B must match what you type into Apple / Google / a vendor form, character for character.
  • Status: the record should be active and its details current. Outdated details can be corrected through D&B's D-U-N-S Manager or support.

If the lookup returns nothing — or the wrong company

Try the full legal name exactly as registered, historical names, and alternate English transliterations. If a similarly named company appears, do not use its number; a wrong entity is worse than no number. If there is genuinely no record, you are in the "new record" path: D&B must create and authenticate it, which takes up to 30 business days free or about 8 business days with D&B's paid expedite.

Do not fire off a duplicate application while an earlier request is pending. Duplicate or conflicting requests can freeze the case for days and may create messy records that later cause Apple verification failures.

What to send someone who is helping you

To make a lookup actually work, prepare: the exact registered legal name (and its English rendering), registered address with postcode, business phone, country, and what you need the number for. The last item matters because Apple, Google Play and procurement portals each validate slightly different fields.

Frequently asked questions

Is the D-U-N-S lookup free?

Yes. D&B offers a free lookup tool, and Apple's lookup during organization enrollment is free too. Third-party services charge for convenience, verification and support.

Why can't I find my company in the D&B database?

Common reasons are a name that differs from the registered version, an outdated address or phone, a very new company, or limited D&B coverage in your jurisdiction. Try searching by address and phone.

Can one company have more than one D-U-N-S Number?

Each physical location of a business can have its own number. One legal entity at one address should normally have one.

Can I use a D-U-N-S Number I found on another company's website?

No. It identifies that legal entity only, and platform verification will reject a mismatch.

My D&B record has the wrong address. What now?

Request an update through D&B's D-U-N-S Manager or contact D&B support. Allow time for the change to propagate before submitting it to Apple or Google.
