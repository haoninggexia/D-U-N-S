D-U-N-S Number for Google Play Developer Accounts

Google verifies organization developers against the same Dun & Bradstreet database Apple uses. If you are registering a Play Console account under a company name, here is what gets checked and how to avoid a stalled verification.

D-U-N-S Helper · Updated November 2026 · 5 min read

Does Google Play require a D-U-N-S Number?

For organization developer accounts, Google asks for a D-U-N-S Number as part of verifying that the business behind the account is real. Personal / individual developer accounts do not need one. Google has been tightening identity verification for organization accounts over the last few years, so a D-U-N-S Number is now a practical prerequisite for publishing under a company name.

As with Apple, the number is not issued by Google. It comes from the Dun & Bradstreet database, and Google validates the details you supply against that record.

What gets verified

  • Legal entity name — must match the D&B record and your registration documents.
  • Registered address — a D-U-N-S Number identifies an entity at a specific location; a mismatch here is a common failure.
  • Contact details — a working business email and phone that can be reached during verification.
  • Entity type — a real legal entity, not a trade name, DBA or branch.

Because Google and Apple both read from the same D&B record, fixing your D&B profile once fixes both. Conversely, a stale record will cause the same rejection on both stores.

Why verification fails

  1. The English legal name in Play Console differs from the D&B record (translation, abbreviation, pinyin, "Ltd" vs "Co., Ltd").
  2. The registered address changed and D&B still holds the old one.
  3. The number belongs to a parent, a branch, or a second office.
  4. The record is brand new and not yet visible to Google's check.
  5. The business phone goes unanswered during a verification call.

Repeatedly resubmitting with slightly different spellings tends to make things worse. Fix the underlying D&B record, then submit once with the exact values.

The practical sequence

Look up your existing record first. If it exists, copy the legal name and address from the D&B record into Play Console rather than the other way round. If it is out of date, request an update from D&B and wait for propagation. Only if no record exists should you submit a new application — and then use D&B's official form with details copied verbatim from your registration documents.

Frequently asked questions

Do individual Google Play developers need a D-U-N-S Number?

No, it applies to organization/company developer accounts.

Can I use the same D-U-N-S Number for Apple and Google Play?

Yes. Both platforms validate against the same D&B database. One number per legal entity per location serves both.

Why does Google say my D-U-N-S Number is invalid?

Most often the legal name or registered address in Play Console does not exactly match the D&B record, or the number belongs to a different entity/location.

How long does Google Play organization verification take?

It is a separate review from D&B. Getting the number is the long pole: minutes if a record exists, up to 30 business days if a new one must be created. Allow extra time for Google's own check.

Is the D-U-N-S Number free?

Yes, D&B issues it free. Any cost is for expedited processing or third-party lookup and assistance.
